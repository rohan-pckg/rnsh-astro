I didn't start building because I had something to say.

I started because I couldn't stop tinkering. A broken WordPress theme at 14.
A half-finished Chrome extension that nobody used. A portfolio site I rebuilt
six times before anyone ever visited it.

At some point the tinkering became something closer to obsession - not with
shipping, but with the *feel* of a thing. The way a transition lands. The
moment an interface stops feeling like software and starts feeling like thought.

## The gap that won't close

There's a gap between what you can imagine and what you can build. Early on,
that gap is enormous and obvious. You know exactly what you want - you just
don't have the vocabulary yet. The tools feel foreign. Every attempt comes out
wrong.

Then slowly, the gap narrows.

You learn the vocabulary. The tools stop fighting you. Things come out *close*
to what you imagined. And that's when it gets dangerous - because *close* is
somehow more frustrating than *far*. You can see exactly where it falls short.
You know what's missing. The gap is smaller but it feels bigger.

I think this is why builders never stop building. It's not productivity. It's
not ambition. It's that the gap never fully closes - and closing it, even a
little, feels like the best thing in the world.

## Motion as meaning

I got pulled into motion almost by accident. I was trying to make something
feel *alive* - not animated for the sake of it, but responsive in the way
a good conversation is responsive. Present. Aware.

Good motion doesn't decorate an interface. It explains it. It tells you where
something came from, where it's going, why it matters. A well-timed transition
carries more information than a tooltip ever could.

I'm still learning this. I suspect I always will be.

## What I'm actually chasing

I think what I'm after is the moment someone uses something I built and
doesn't think about it at all - they just *do the thing*. The interface
disappears. The friction is gone. They're just thinking, or creating, or
connecting.

That disappearing act is the hardest thing to pull off. It requires getting
a hundred small decisions right: the spacing, the timing, the hierarchy, the
words, the flow. Any one of them slightly off and the spell breaks.

I don't always get there. I rarely get there. But occasionally something
clicks into place and it feels exactly right - and that's enough to keep going.

So I keep building. Experimenting. Disappearing into the lab.

The gap won't close. That's fine.
